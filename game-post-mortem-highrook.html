    Game Post-mortem - Highrook

    A rocky road to Highrook

    So "The Horror At Highrook" has been out for five months now and I am in that in-between-projects phase where I am trying out various prototypes and putting together demos in order to test out ideas. Along with scribbling down design notes and mocking up scenes I have had some time to reflect on my previous game and think about how it might effect my future choices.

    Highrook was a game that exist partly due to a change in games industry funding. In  2022/23 I was working on a deep content rich tactical strategy game called Void Warden. I had a substantial demo and I began pitching around to try and get the money to finish the project. I had already spent a big chunk of my own cash on the prototype and I knew that I didn't have enough money in the company accounts to finish making the game on my own dime. Unfortunately, after almost 6 months of pitching and negotiations with various publishers It became clear that something was up. 

    Industry Woes

    Games were still being signed, but publishers had become a lot more wary and they were looking more towards low cost breakout-meme types of projects. Either that or much larger team budgets 1million+. The mid range I project/budget was pitching wasn't being funded, or at least the opportunities for that scale of game had shrunk massively compared to a few years before. I could have carried on pitching in the hope of eventually getting a deal that worked (I had already turned down a few deals that I wasn't happy with) OR I needed to pivot to something else. If I was going to change track it had to be to a project I thought I could finish within my remaining company finances.

    The game that fitted this requirement and was most advanced in terms of prototyping was Highrook. It was a game I'd wanted to make for years, and I'd already worked on various iterations of the idea. It took a backseat to my tactics game simply because I felt the audience for Highrook had a fairly low ceiling and it was a genre that would be hard to sell to publishers. But now I was looking to self publish I didn't have to worry so much about the 'industry pitch', however I did need to work out how to reach an audience for the game.

    One man workshop

    Highrook was primarily a solo project, I wrote 100% of the code, wrote 90% of the narrative, drew 100% of the character art. I contracted folk to help with editing, QA, music, and community.
