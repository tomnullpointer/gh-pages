    Trad Roguelike Dev - Survey

    Steam lists approx. 388 matches for the Tags "Traditional + Roguelike".

    Most of these are well known classics (modern classics perhaps) and have been around in one form or another for years. I could also include ADOM, Dungeonmans, Golden Krone Hotel etc here too. Its also worth noting that steams review system wasn't used much a decade or so ago, and older games are more likely to have been bought but not reviewed. Anything with a review count of over 1k I consider a roaring success. Even though most of these games have been out for a long time (decades even) they have still performed pretty well.
     
    However there are a lot of other traditional ascii/simple tile-set TradRLs that have not done so well. I'm not going to list any, but there are a lot of smaller, less polished roguelikes that have very few reviews. In addition many of these games can be listed as "coming soon" for years, and might never be released. Its easy for developers to underestimate the amount of work involved in making a rogulike, despite the simple impression often given by the graphics.
     
    I also have concerns that TradRLs can rely on Early Access periods, a development approach that has seen some backlash recently. However, I think TradRLs are still one of the genres that can justify the approach, due to their complexity and content. 
     
    I feel that the complexity and depth of the genre already appeals to existing fans, but the graphical style and long dev-cycles might be a barrier to gaining more players.
     
    As someone who is both a nerdy systems designed and an artist I wanted to see if many TradRLs had made the move over to more modern interfaces and graphics.

    All of the games listed above have been released in the last 5 years and have review counts of over 1k. They are all either TradRLs or very close in implementation, but exhibit more modern graphics, gameplay and QoL. I suspect that in fact the majority of people playing these games aren't TradRL players but are enjoying them because they offer a experience that is deep and detailed but in a familiar modern framework.
     
    I think this is the area I am most suited to work in, with a background in graphical pcg and systems based games design it feels like a natural direction for my next project.
    I have concerns that the scope of projects in this genre space is often inadvisably large for small teams or solo devs, but part of me sees that as a challenge, and it feels like a design space where I could really bring my experience to bear.
